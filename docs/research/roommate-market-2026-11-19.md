## 1. Demand in Warsaw and Poland

### 1.1 Search volume

- No free public tool returned volumes for "szukam współlokatora", "współlokator Warszawa", "ищу соседа Варшава" or "шукаю сусіда Варшава". Ahrefs' free generator showed no data, and Semrush and Senuto need a login. I found no article that publishes these volumes.
- **Proxy (E):** A page at position ~9 gets an impression on almost every search where it ranks. Passflat's roommate page has ~1,400 impressions a year at position 8.8, so the long-tail queries it ranks for come to roughly **100–120 Google searches a month**. At a typical 1–3% CTR for position 9, that is **15–40 clicks a year**. Ranking higher for the head terms would add volume, but Google alone will not fill a matching pool.
- WG-Gesucht shows **22 people searching for a flatshare in Warsaw in the last 24 hours and 0 offers** [19]. This is a foreign-language niche that nobody serves, and it is small.

A 2026 Randstad guide for foreigners calls Facebook groups "the fastest way" to find direct offers [20]. The founders of mRazem said in 2018 that they had searched **37 Facebook groups** to find a room in Warsaw and counted **250+ such groups** across Poland's 10 largest cities [22]. Demand exists, it is spread thin, and it already lives in free channels.

### 1.3 Seasonality

- July to September is the "traditional peak" [6]. Room demand rises in **late August and early September**, when people who could not rent a whole flat move down to rooms [3].
- In September 2025 Otodom listings drew **604k responses, 12% more than August** and the second-highest level since 2018 [5]. October 2025 had **31% fewer responses** than September [6]. December 2025 demand was **23% below November**, and January usually rebounds [7].
- I found no data for the February semester. Erasmus posts mention stays from "mid-Sept to Feb", so spring arrivals come in late January and February [11] (E: a small secondary peak).
- **Today, 19 November, falls in the low season.** Any test run between now and mid-January starts against the trough.

**What this means:** "Rent a flat together" only works for people with budgets of about 2,400 zł or more, mostly young professionals. Students with ~1,300–1,500 zł look for a room in an existing flat.

Ideal Flatmate (UK, personality matching, £5/week for seekers in 2017 [50]) and Kangaroom (acquired by SpareRoom in 2018 per CB Insights, snippet [49]) are minor. I found no evidence of a product called "Happy Roommates". Searches showed no Facebook Marketplace rentals category for Poland, and Facebook groups dominate there [20]. I found no university-run roommate board in Warsaw. The UW dormitory shortage led to protests in October 2023 [10].

**Lessons from the table:**

1. The winners (SpareRoom, Flatmates.com.au) are **room-listing marketplaces**, where seeker-to-seeker matching is a side feature.
2. Pure "matching" startups either die or earn almost nothing. Badi's €180k revenue against a €10M loss shows this [40].
3. Poland already has 3–4 free attempts with thin pools, and two of them are dead [21–28].

## 4. Cold start for a solo founder

**Published thresholds (none of them for roommate matching):**

| Product   | Threshold                                             |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Tinder    | ~500 people on one campus (USC) [58]                  |
| Airbnb    | 300 listings, 100 of them reviewed [58]               |
| Uber      | 30+ drivers and an ETA under 15 minutes per city [59] |
| OpenTable | 50–100 restaurants per city [59]                      |
| Facebook  | 7 friends in 10 days [58]                             |

16 of the 17 marketplaces Lenny Rachitsky studied started with a geographic or category constraint [59].

**Tactics that worked:**

- **One place first.** Badi launched only in Barcelona [41], Bunky at one university [51], Tinder at USC [58].
- **Events that make the news.** SpareRoom's speed flatmating events [34][35].
- **Messaging users can act on.** At SpareRoom, messaging was the feature that took off [34].
- **Telegram works for Polish rental tools.** FON reports 3,000+ active users with interfaces in UA, PL and EN [53]. Flatty's roommate group for Minsk reached 2,264 members [18].
- **Counterexample.** Flatmejt launched with Gumtree's backing and no pool, and it is gone [21][23].

**Minimum density for Passflat (E).** A seeker only finds a match useful if another person's move-in window (±1 month), budget band, gender preference, language and district all fit. If each of these filters keeps roughly 35–60% of the pool, the combined share is about 5%. Seeing 5–10 compatible people then needs **~100–200 active profiles in Warsaw**, counting only profiles created or renewed in the last 30 days. The pool can be smaller if it sticks to one segment, for example English-speaking young professionals with budgets ≥2,400 zł who want to rent a whole flat together.

## 5. Risks

- **Facebook.** Meta's spam policy targets posting to groups "at very high frequencies", manual or automated, and also covers misleading links (snippet) [62]. Identical copy across many groups is "the single strongest spam signal you control", and a block can follow the link's domain rather than the account [61]. That puts passflat.com itself at risk.
- **Telegram.** Unsolicited links and ads get reported. A first limit lasts "a few days", repeated limits get longer, and continued spam can block messaging strangers permanently [60].
- **Reputation.** Spamming rental groups is exactly what Roomster was accused of [39][37]. Admins remove link-only posts.
- **Scams in Polish rental search:** below-market prices, an owner "abroad", time pressure and a 500–1,500 zł deposit before any viewing; ID scans requested "for the contract" and reused for fraud [63]; "booking deposits" by BLIK and fake bank-login links [20]. A roommate product imports all of these through fake "I have a room" profiles.
- **Payments.** From mid-November 2026, Telegram allows bots to sell digital services **only in Stars**; card payments inside the app are barred [64].
- **Structural problems:** the need comes once every 1–2 years, a successful match makes the user leave, free groups with 100k+ members already exist, demand is seasonal, and Passflat's room-listing side got zero offers since June 2026.
- **Legal (not researched here; check before launch):** GDPR for profile data (collect nothing sensitive, auto-expire), DSA notice-and-action for user content, 18+ only.

## 6. Judgement (labelled as judgement)

**Is it viable at Passflat's scale?** It is not viable as a standalone business. It is viable only as a cheap, time-boxed feature test. Pure matching has a poor record: Badi, the Roommates app, Roomi, Flatmejt and mRazem all failed or shrank. Polish attempts already exist and have thin pools. At 5–9 Google clicks a week, organic search adds almost nothing, so the pool depends entirely on the founder's two 30-minute posting slots. What argues for a test:

- Passflat already has a Telegram bot.
- Its rent and utility figures for each building and district give it a hook nobody else has: "what two of you can afford together, and where".
- RU/UA/EN speakers in Warsaw have no roommate-specific chat (§1.2).

**The smallest version worth testing (1–2 weeks to build):**

1. A profile in the Telegram bot and on a web page, with seven fields: type (rent a whole flat together / I have a room in my flat / looking for a room), budget, districts, move-in month, gender and preference, languages, one-line bio.
2. Matching by double opt-in that reveals Telegram usernames. No chat of its own.
3. A daily digest of new compatible profiles.
4. Profiles expire after 30 days unless the user renews them with one tap.
5. A public counter by district and budget, for example "17 people looking in Mokotów ≤1,800 zł for February". It serves as the waitlist and as content worth sharing.
6. A pair calculator that draws on Passflat's building rents.
7. Scam rules from day one: never ask for money or an ID, an in-bot report button, ban any profile that mentions deposits.
8. Segment focus: English, Russian and Ukrainian speakers, plus young professionals renting together.
9. Posting plan: useful district rent figures with the link, varied wording, no more than 3–4 groups per slot, admins asked first.

**Timing:** Build now. Start posting around 1 December with "matching opens 7 January for February move-ins". Judge at the end of January. A failure in the low season is a weaker kill signal than one in August–September, so if the result is borderline, rerun the test from 1 August 2027.

**Metric and thresholds:**

| Check                                                      | Proves it                                                               | Kills it   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Inflow from 2×30 min/day (week 3–4)                        | ≥40 completed Warsaw profiles/week                                      | <15/week   |
| Density (week 6)                                           | ≥150 active profiles, with ≥3 district × budget cells of ≥10 profiles   | <60 active |
| **Core: mutual opt-in rate** (profiles older than 14 days) | ≥30% have at least one mutual match                                     | <15%       |
| Outcome (bot follow-up, week 8)                            | ≥10 people confirm "found someone via Passflat"                         | <3         |
| Self-propagation (week 8)                                  | ≥20% of new profiles arrive from sources other than the founder's posts | <5%        |

**Money without staff (all estimates):**

1. Charge for speed, never for contact. Instant alerts, "see who opted in to you" and a top slot in digests could cost 9.99–14.99 zł a week or 29.99 zł a month, in line with BotOLX and FON. Sell them through Telegram Stars inside the bot, or BLIK/Przelewy24 on the website. Check Telegram's rules before linking out from the bot.
2. Expect little at test scale. 150 active profiles × 4% paying × ~40 zł comes to **~240 zł a month**. Even at FON's claimed 3,000 active users, 5% paying gives ~6,750 zł a month.
3. Later, without staff:
   - Affiliate offers at the moment a pair forms (movers, internet, insurance). I did not research the commissions.
   - Paid "verified pair" leads for landlords of two-room flats. This moves back toward the listings marketplace the founder rejected, so treat it as optional.

The realistic value is traffic and returning users for Passflat's rent data, not direct revenue.
